# Vocabulary addendum — handover to Claude Design

Date: 2026-09-20 · Supersedes the „Vocabulary" row in §1 and §3G of `handover-claude-design-2026-09-19.md`.
The earlier decision „full in-world vocabulary, actions included" is **withdrawn**. The concepts as drawn are too deep in their theme to be understood: a person cannot find the shelf, the sessions or the winner when every design calls them something else.

## The rule in one sentence

**The nouns and the navigation are Spielwirbel's. The ritual is the theme's.**

A word that names a thing a user might look for, or say aloud to someone at the table, is the Spielwirbel word in every design. The theme lives in what a screen looks like, in the moment of starting and voting, and in decoration that carries no meaning a user needs.

## 3. Constraints on the themed places

- **Self-explanatory from the screen.** A themed word must be understood without the glossary: the vessel is drawn, the primary button sits where and looks like the primary action, the vote card shows the faces and 1–5. If a themed word needs the glossary to be understood, use the standard word.
- **Same policy for all six designs.** A design may use *less* theme than allowed, never more. Klassisch uses none. Tisch, Ocean and Forest are expected to stay close to the standard words even where theme is allowed; Brücke and Run carry most of it.
- **Translatable.** Deliver DE + EN; we translate seven more locales, so a themed word needs an obvious equivalent (Zündung → Ignition, Schub → Thrust). No untranslatable puns.
- **Never an evening word for a session** (Abend, Spielabend, game night, tonight as a name) — a test bans those in every locale.
- **The glossary shrinks to the themed places.** Per design, one short section: the pot word, the two action verbs, the vote question and scale ends, the reveal verb. Nothing else needs explaining, because nothing else is renamed.
